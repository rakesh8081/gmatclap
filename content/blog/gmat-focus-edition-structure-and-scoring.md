The GMAT Focus Edition is the current version of the GMAT. If you are starting your preparation now, this is the exam you will take. Here is the structure at a glance.

## The three sections

| Section | Time | Questions | Score range |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quantitative Reasoning | 45 min | 21 | 60–90 |
| Verbal Reasoning | 45 min | 23 | 60–90 |
| Data Insights | 45 min | 20 | 60–90 |

In total that is **64 questions in 2 hours 15 minutes**, with an overall score from **205 to 805**.

## What each section tests

### Quantitative Reasoning

- Algebra
- Arithmetic
- Number system
- Statistics
- Permutations, combinations and probability

Every question is problem solving. There is no geometry, and no calculator in this section.

### Verbal Reasoning

- Critical Reasoning
- Reading Comprehension

Sentence Correction is no longer part of the exam, so verbal preparation is now about reasoning and reading, not grammar rules.

### Data Insights

- Data Sufficiency
- Multi-Source Reasoning
- Table Analysis
- Graphics Interpretation
- Two-Part Analysis

Data Insights mixes maths with reading and logic. An on-screen calculator is available in this section.

## Three things that help on test day

- **You choose the section order.** Many students start with their strongest section to build confidence; others get their weakest out of the way first. Try both in mock tests.
- **You can review and change answers.** You can bookmark questions and change up to three answers per section before time runs out, so a guess under pressure does not have to be final.
- **Every section counts equally.** Quant, Verbal and Data Insights all contribute to the 205–805 total, so you cannot ignore Data Insights the way many people once ignored the old Integrated Reasoning section.
